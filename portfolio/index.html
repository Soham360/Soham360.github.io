Club & Team Work

UW · Aerospace

Society for Advanced Rocket Propulsion

Machining aluminum propulsion hardware and developing 3D-printed tooling for composite structural couplers.

Role Manufacturing
Tools & Techniques Manual Milling · 3D Printed Tooling · Carbon Fiber & Fiberglass Layups
Since October 2025

Overview

I am a member of the manufacturing team at the University of Washington's Society for Advanced Rocket Propulsion (SARP). The team builds structural and propulsion hardware for high-power rocket systems, and my work covers precision machining and composite manufacturing research.

Machining and engineering drawings

I work from engineering drawings to machine custom aluminum parts on a manual mill. Stock is cut to length on the band saw, then set up and machined to size. Before assembly, I check each part against the tolerances on the drawing so the finished hardware is ready to fly.

Composite coupler development

I also research tooling processes for the rocket's structural couplers. The work uses 3D-printed molds to lay up carbon fiber and fiberglass parts. I vary mold geometry, surface preparation, release agents, and layup technique, with the goal of producing lightweight, high-strength structural joints.

UW · Structural Fabrication

UW Steel Bridge

Fabrication and timed assembly of a scale steel bridge for the University of Washington AISC Steel Bridge team, which placed 2nd overall at the regional competition and 18th at nationals.

Role Construction Subteam & Timed Builder
Tools Welding · Milling · Rapid Assembly
Since October 2025

Overview

I am a member of the construction subteam, which fabricates and assembles the team's bridge for regional and national intercollegiate competitions. The bridge is built to the competition rulebook and judged on both assembly time and structural performance.

Fabrication and assembly

My work includes welding and machining steel structural members to specification, fitting parts in the shop, and rehearsing rapid assembly. When parts do not come together as the drawings predicted, the subteam reworks them. At competition I served on the official build team and assembled the bridge on the floor within the competition's timing rules and tolerances.

Competition results

At the regional competition hosted by the University of Alaska Anchorage, the team placed 2nd overall and qualified for the national finals. Category results were:

  • 2nd place: construction speed
  • 2nd place: lightness
  • 2nd place: stiffness
  • 2nd place: efficiency
  • 2nd place: economy
  • 4th place: aesthetics

At the AISC Student Steel Bridge Competition National Finals at the University of Texas at El Paso, the team placed 18th nationally.

Design / CAD

Phone Holder

A multi-part phone holder modeled and tested as a full moving assembly in SolidWorks.

Category Mechanical Design
Tools SolidWorks · 3D Printing
Status Completed ✔

Overview

This project focused on assembly design. The holder has several components that must fit and move correctly relative to one another, so I modeled each part separately and then built them into a complete assembly.

Design and validation

I assembled the mechanism in SolidWorks and tested its motion to confirm it worked as intended before rendering the animation shown above. The parts are designed for 3D printing.

Application

The cutout in the holder is engineered to fit an Anker MagGo 622 power bank, which allows the phone to charge while mounted. The resulting geometry also holds the phone at a suitable angle for watching video in landscape.

Design / CAD

Racing Sim Wheel

A 3D-printed sim racing steering wheel built around a magnetic rotary encoder and a microcontroller that reports steering position to a PC over USB.

Category Mechanical / Electronics
Tools SolidWorks · Arduino · 3D Printing
Status In Progress

Overview

The goal is a functional wheel for PC racing games, so the design is built around delivering steering data to the computer over USB. The project combines mechanical design, CAD, fabrication, electronics, and embedded programming.

Mechanical design

A steel shaft runs through bearings at the center of the wheel, and the surrounding structure is 3D printed. The main design constraint was a compact mechanism that still allows roughly 540 degrees of rotation.

Electronics

A magnetic rotary encoder tracks the position of the shaft. A microcontroller reads the encoder and reports that position to the computer over USB.
